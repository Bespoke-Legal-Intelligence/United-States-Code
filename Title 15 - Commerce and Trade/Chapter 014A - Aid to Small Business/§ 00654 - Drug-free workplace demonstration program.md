### §654. Drug-free workplace demonstration program

#### (a) Definitions

In this section:

#### (1) Drug-free workplace program

&emsp;The term “drug-free workplace program” means a program that includes—

&emsp;&emsp;(A) a written policy, including a clear statement of expectations for workplace behavior, prohibitions against reporting to work or working under the influence of illegal drugs or alcohol, prohibitions against the use or possession of illegal drugs in the workplace, and the consequences of violating those expectations and prohibitions;

&emsp;&emsp;(B) drug and alcohol abuse prevention training for a total of not less than 2 hours for each employee, and additional voluntary drug and alcohol abuse prevention training for employees who are parents;

&emsp;&emsp;(C) employee illegal drug testing, with analysis conducted by a drug testing laboratory certified by the Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration, or approved by the College of American Pathologists for forensic drug testing, and a review of each positive test result by a medical review officer;

&emsp;&emsp;(D) employee access to an employee assistance program, including confidential assessment, referral, and short-term problem resolution; and

&emsp;&emsp;(E) continuing alcohol and drug abuse prevention education.

#### (2) Eligible intermediary

&emsp;The term “eligible intermediary” means an organization—

&emsp;&emsp;(A) that has not less than 2 years of experience in carrying out drug-free workplace programs;

&emsp;&emsp;(B) that has a drug-free workplace policy in effect;

&emsp;&emsp;(C) that is located in a State, the District of Columbia, or a territory of the United States; and

&emsp;&emsp;(D) the purpose of which is—

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(i) to develop comprehensive drug-free workplace programs or to supply drug-free workplace services; or

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(ii) to provide other forms of assistance and services to small business concerns.

#### (3) Employee

&emsp;The term “employee” includes any—

&emsp;&emsp;(A) applicant for employment;

&emsp;&emsp;(B) employee;

&emsp;&emsp;(C) supervisor;

&emsp;&emsp;(D) manager;

&emsp;&emsp;(E) officer of a small business concern who is active in management of the concern; and

&emsp;&emsp;(F) owner of a small business concern who is active in management of the concern.

#### (4) Medical review officer

&emsp;The term “medical review officer”—

&emsp;&emsp;(A) means a licensed physician with knowledge of substance abuse disorders; and

&emsp;&emsp;(B) does not include any—

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(i) employee of the small business concern; or

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(ii) employee or agent of, or any person having a financial interest in, the laboratory for which the illegal drug test results are being reviewed.

#### (b) Establishment

There is established a drug-free workplace demonstration program, under which the Administrator may make grants to, or enter into cooperative agreements or contracts with, eligible intermediaries for the purpose of providing financial and technical assistance to small business concerns seeking to establish a drug-free workplace program.

#### (c) Privacy protection for employees participating in drug-free workplace program

Each drug-free workplace program established with assistance made available under this section shall—

&emsp;(1) include, as reasonably necessary and appropriate, practices and procedures to ensure the confidentiality of illegal drug test results and of any participation by an employee in a rehabilitation program;

&emsp;(2) prohibit the mandatory disclosure of medical information by an employee prior to a confirmed positive illegal drug test; and

&emsp;(3) require that a medical review officer reviewing illegal drug test results shall report only the final results, limited to those drugs for which the employee tests positive, in writing and in a manner designed to ensure the confidentiality of the results.

#### (d) Evaluation and coordination

Not later than 18 months after October 21, 1998, the Administrator, in coordination with the Secretary of Labor, the Secretary of Health and Human Services, and the Director of National Drug Control Policy, shall—

&emsp;(1) evaluate the drug-free workplace programs established with assistance made available under this section; and

&emsp;(2) submit to Congress a report describing the results of the evaluation under paragraph (1).

#### (e) Contract authority

In carrying out this section, the Administrator may—

&emsp;(1) contract with public and private entities to provide assistance related to carrying out the program under this section; and

&emsp;(2) compensate those entities for provision of that assistance.

#### (f) Construction

Nothing in this section may be construed to require an employer who attends a program offered by an intermediary to contract for any service offered by the intermediary.

#### (g) Authorization

#### (1) In general

&emsp;There is authorized to be appropriated to carry out this section, $10,000,000 for fiscal years 1999 and 2000. Amounts made available under this subsection shall remain available until expended.

#### (2) Small business development centers

&emsp;Of the total amount made available under this subsection, not more than the greater of 10 percent or $1,000,000 may be used to carry out section 648(c)(3)(T) of this title.

#### Amendments

1998—Pub. L. 105–277 amended section catchline and text generally. Prior to amendment, text consisted of subsecs. (a) to (c) authorizing Administration to make grants to conduct tourism demonstration programs, establishing purpose of program, placing a condition on grant recipients, authorizing appropriations, and requiring report to President and Congress.

#### Findings and Purposes of 1998 Amendment

Pub. L. 105–277, div. C, title IX, §902, Oct. 21, 1998, 112 Stat. 2681–707, provided that:

“(a)

Findings.—Congress finds that—

&emsp;“(1) 74 percent of adults who use illegal drugs are employed;

&emsp;“(2) small business concerns employ over 50 percent of the Nation's workforce;

&emsp;“(3) in more than 88 percent of families with children under the age of 18, at least 1 parent is employed; and

&emsp;“(4) employees who use and abuse addictive illegal drugs and alcohol increase costs for businesses and risk the health and safety of all employees because—

&emsp;&emsp;“(A) absenteeism is 66 percent higher among drug users than individuals who do not use drugs;

&emsp;&emsp;“(B) health benefit utilization is 300 percent higher among drug users than individuals who do not use drugs;

&emsp;&emsp;“(C) 47 percent of workplace accidents are drug-related;

&emsp;&emsp;“(D) disciplinary actions are 90 percent higher among drug users than among individuals who do not use drugs; and

&emsp;&emsp;“(E) employee turnover is significantly higher among drug users than among individuals who do not use drugs.

“(b)

Purposes.—The purposes of this title \[see Short Title of 1998 Amendment note set out under section 631 of this title\] are to—

&emsp;“(1) educate small business concerns about the advantages of a drug-free workplace;

&emsp;“(2) provide grants and technical assistance in addition to financial incentives to enable small business concerns to create a drug-free workplace;

&emsp;“(3) assist working parents in keeping their children drug-free; and

&emsp;“(4) encourage small business employers and employees alike to participate in drug-free workplace programs.”

#### Sense of Congress for 1998 Amendment

Pub. L. 105–277, div. C, title IX, §903, Oct. 21, 1998, 112 Stat. 2681–708, provided that: “It is the sense of Congress that—

&emsp;“(1) businesses should adopt drug-free workplace programs;

&emsp;“(2) States should consider incentives to encourage businesses to adopt drug-free workplace programs; and

&emsp;“(3) such incentives may include—

&emsp;&emsp;“(A) financial incentives, including—

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;“(i) a reduction in workers’ compensation premiums;

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;“(ii) a reduction in unemployment insurance premiums; and

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;“(iii) tax deductions in an amount equal to the amount of expenditures for employee assistance programs, treatment, or illegal drug testing; and

&emsp;&emsp;“(B) other incentives, such as the adoption of liability limitations, as recommended by the President's Commission on Model State Drug Laws.”
