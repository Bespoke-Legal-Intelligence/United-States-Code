### §3104. Reports on security clearances

#### (a) Report on security clearance determinations

(1) Not later than February 1 of each year, the President shall submit to Congress a report on the security clearance process. Such report shall include, for each security clearance level—

&emsp;(A) the number of employees of the United States Government who—

&emsp;&emsp;(i) held a security clearance at such level as of October 1 of the preceding year; and

&emsp;&emsp;(ii) were approved for a security clearance at such level during the preceding fiscal year;

&emsp;(B) the number of contractors to the United States Government who—

&emsp;&emsp;(i) held a security clearance at such level as of October 1 of the preceding year; and

&emsp;&emsp;(ii) were approved for a security clearance at such level during the preceding fiscal year; and

&emsp;(C) for each element of the intelligence community—

&emsp;&emsp;(i) the total amount of time it took to process the security clearance determination for such level that—

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(I) was among the 80 percent of security clearance determinations made during the preceding fiscal year that took the shortest amount of time to complete; and

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(II) took the longest amount of time to complete;

&emsp;&emsp;(ii) the total amount of time it took to process the security clearance determination for such level that—

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(I) was among the 90 percent of security clearance determinations made during the preceding fiscal year that took the shortest amount of time to complete; and

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(II) took the longest amount of time to complete;

&emsp;&emsp;(iii) the number of pending security clearance investigations for such level as of October 1 of the preceding year that have remained pending for—

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(I) 4 months or less;

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(II) between 4 months and 8 months;

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(III) between 8 months and one year; and

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(IV) more than one year;

&emsp;&emsp;(iv) the percentage of reviews during the preceding fiscal year that resulted in a denial or revocation of a security clearance;

&emsp;&emsp;(v) the percentage of investigations during the preceding fiscal year that resulted in incomplete information;

&emsp;&emsp;(vi) the percentage of investigations during the preceding fiscal year that did not result in enough information to make a decision on potentially adverse information; and

&emsp;&emsp;(vii) for security clearance determinations completed or pending during the preceding fiscal year that have taken longer than one year to complete—

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(I) the number of security clearance determinations for positions as employees of the United States Government that required more than one year to complete;

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(II) the number of security clearance determinations for contractors that required more than one year to complete;

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(III) the agencies that investigated and adjudicated such determinations; and

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(IV) the cause of significant delays in such determinations.

(2) For purposes of paragraph (1), the President may consider—

&emsp;(A) security clearances at the level of confidential and secret as one security clearance level; and

&emsp;(B) security clearances at the level of top secret or higher as one security clearance level.

#### (b) Form

The reports required under subsection (a)(1) shall be submitted in unclassified form, but may include a classified annex.
