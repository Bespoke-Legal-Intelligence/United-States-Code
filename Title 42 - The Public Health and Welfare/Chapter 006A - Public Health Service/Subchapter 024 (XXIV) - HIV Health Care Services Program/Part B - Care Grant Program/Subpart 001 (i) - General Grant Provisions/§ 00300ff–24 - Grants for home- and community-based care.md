### §300ff–24. Grants for home- and community-based care

#### (a) Uses

A State may use amounts provided under a grant awarded under this part to make grants under section 300ff–22(a)(2) <sup>1</sup> of this title to entities to—

&emsp;(1) provide home- and community-based health services for individuals with HIV disease pursuant to written plans of care prepared by a case management team, that shall include appropriate health care professionals, in such State for providing such services to such individuals;

&emsp;(2) provide outreach services to individuals with HIV disease, including those individuals in rural areas; and

&emsp;(3) provide for the coordination of the provision of services under this section with the provision of HIV-related health services provided by public and private entities.

#### (b) Priority

In awarding grants under subsection (a) of this section, a State shall give priority to entities that provide assurances to the State that—

&emsp;(1) such entities will participate in HIV care consortia if such consortia exist within the State; and

&emsp;(2) such entities will utilize amounts provided under such grants for the provision of home- and community-based services to low-income individuals with HIV disease.

#### (c) “Home- and community-based health services” defined

As used in this part, the term “home- and community-based health services”—

&emsp;(1) means, with respect to an individual with HIV disease, skilled health services furnished to the individual in the individual's home pursuant to a written plan of care established by a case management team, that shall include appropriate health care professionals, for the provision of such services and items described in paragraph (2);

&emsp;(2) includes—

&emsp;&emsp;(A) durable medical equipment;

&emsp;&emsp;(B) homemaker or home health aide services and personal care services furnished in the home of the individual;

&emsp;&emsp;(C) day treatment or other partial hospitalization services;

&emsp;&emsp;(D) home intravenous and aerosolized drug therapy (including prescription drugs administered as part of such therapy);

&emsp;&emsp;(E) routine diagnostic testing administered in the home of the individual; and

&emsp;&emsp;(F) appropriate mental health, developmental, and rehabilitation services; and

&emsp;(3) does not include—

&emsp;&emsp;(A) inpatient hospital services; and

&emsp;&emsp;(B) nursing home and other long term care facilities.

(July 1, 1944, ch. 373, title XXVI, §2614, as added Pub. L. 101–381, title II, §201, Aug. 18, 1990, 104 Stat. 589.)
