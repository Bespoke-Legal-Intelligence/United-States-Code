### §11901. Congressional findings

The Congress finds that—

&emsp;(1) the Federal Government has a duty to provide public and other federally assisted low-income housing that is decent, safe, and free from illegal drugs;

&emsp;(2) public and other federally assisted low-income housing in many areas suffers from rampant drug-related crime;

&emsp;(3) drug dealers are increasingly imposing a reign of terror on public and other federally assisted low-income housing tenants;

&emsp;(4) the increase in drug-related crime not only leads to murders, muggings, and other forms of violence against tenants, but also to a deterioration of the physical environment that requires substantial government expenditures; and

&emsp;(5) local law enforcement authorities often lack the resources to deal with the drug problem in public and other federally assisted low-income housing, particularly in light of the recent reductions in Federal aid to cities.

(Pub. L. 100–690, title V, §5122, Nov. 18, 1988, 102 Stat. 4301; Pub. L. 101–625, title V, §581(a), Nov. 28, 1990, 104 Stat. 4245.)

#### Amendments

1990—Pub. L. 101–625 amended section generally. Prior to amendment, section read as follows: “The Congress finds that—

&emsp;“(1) the Federal Government has a duty to provide public housing that is decent, safe, and free from illegal drugs;

&emsp;“(2) public housing projects in many areas suffer from rampant drug-related crime;

&emsp;“(3) drug dealers are increasingly imposing a reign of terror on public housing tenants;

&emsp;“(4) the increase in drug-related crime not only leads to murders, muggings, and other forms of violence against tenants, but also to a deterioration of the physical environment that requires substantial government expenditures; and

&emsp;“(5) local law enforcement authorities often lack the resources to deal with the drug problem in public housing, particularly in light of the recent reductions in Federal aid to cities.”
