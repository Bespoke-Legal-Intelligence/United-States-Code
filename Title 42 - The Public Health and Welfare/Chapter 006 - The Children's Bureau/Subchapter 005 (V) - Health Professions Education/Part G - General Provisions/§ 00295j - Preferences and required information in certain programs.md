### §295j. Preferences and required information in certain programs

#### (a) Preferences in making awards

#### (1) In general

&emsp;Subject to paragraph (2), in making awards of grants or contracts under any of sections 293k through 293<i>o</i> of this title, under section 294b of this title, or under section 294d or 294e of this title, the Secretary shall give preference to any qualified applicant that—

&emsp;&emsp;(A) has a high rate for placing graduates in practice settings having the principal focus of serving residents of medically underserved communities; or

&emsp;&emsp;(B) during the 2-year period preceding the fiscal year for which such an award is sought, has achieved a significant increase in the rate of placing graduates in such settings.

#### (2) Limitation regarding peer review

&emsp;For purposes of paragraph (1), the Secretary may not give an applicant preference if the proposal of the applicant is ranked at or below the 20th percentile of proposals that have been recommended for approval by peer review groups under section 295<i>o</i>(a) of this title.

#### (b) Required submission of information

The Secretary may make an award of a grant or contract under any of sections 293k through 293<i>o</i> of this title or under section 294b, 294d, or 294e of this title only if the applicant for the award submits to the Secretary (through the application required in section 295<i>o</i>(f)(2) of this title) the following information regarding the programs of the applicant:

&emsp;(1) A description of rotations or preceptorships for students, or clinical training programs for residents, that have the principal focus of providing health care to medically underserved communities.

&emsp;(2) The number of faculty on admissions committees who have a clinical practice in community-based ambulatory settings in medically underserved communities.

&emsp;(3) With respect to individuals who are from disadvantaged backgrounds or from medically underserved communities, the number of such individuals who are recruited for academic programs of the applicant, the number of such individuals who are admitted to such programs, and the number of such individuals who graduate from such programs.

&emsp;(4) If applicable, the number of recent graduates who have chosen careers in primary health care.

&emsp;(5) The number of recent graduates whose practices are serving medically underserved communities.

&emsp;(6) A description of whether and to what extent the applicant is able to operate without Federal assistance under this subchapter.

#### (c) “Graduate” defined

For purposes of this section, the term “graduate” means, unless otherwise specified, an individual who has successfully completed all training and residency requirements necessary for full certification in the health profession selected by the individual.

#### Required Assurances Regarding Bloodborne Diseases

Section 308 of Pub. L. 102–408 provided that: “With respect to awards of grants or contracts under title VII or VIII of the Public Health Service Act \[this subchapter or subchapter VI of this chapter\], the Secretary of Health and Human Services may make such an award for the provision of traineeships only if the applicant for the award provides assurances satisfactory to the Secretary that all trainees will, as appropriate, receive instruction in the utilization of universal precautions and infection control procedures for the prevention of the transmission of bloodborne diseases.”
