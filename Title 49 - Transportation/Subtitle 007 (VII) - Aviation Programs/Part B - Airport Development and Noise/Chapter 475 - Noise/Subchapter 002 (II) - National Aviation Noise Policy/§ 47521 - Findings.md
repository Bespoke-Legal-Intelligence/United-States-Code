### §47521. Findings

Congress finds that—

&emsp;(1) aviation noise management is crucial to the continued increase in airport capacity;

&emsp;(2) community noise concerns have led to uncoordinated and inconsistent restrictions on aviation that could impede the national air transportation system;

&emsp;(3) a noise policy must be carried out at the national level;

&emsp;(4) local interest in aviation noise management shall be considered in determining the national interest;

&emsp;(5) community concerns can be alleviated through the use of new technology aircraft and the use of revenues, including those available from passenger facility charges, for noise management;

&emsp;(6) revenues controlled by the United States Government can help resolve noise problems and carry with them a responsibility to the national airport system;

&emsp;(7) revenues derived from a passenger facility charge may be applied to noise management and increased airport capacity; and

&emsp;(8) a precondition to the establishment and collection of a passenger facility charge is the prescribing by the Secretary of Transportation of a regulation establishing procedures for reviewing airport noise and access restrictions on operations of stage 2 and stage 3 aircraft.

(Pub. L. 103–272, §1(e), July 5, 1994, 108 Stat. 1287; Pub. L. 112–95, title I, §111(c)(2)(A)(vi), (B), Feb. 14, 2012, 126 Stat. 18.)
