### §115. Personnel strengths: requirement for annual authorization

(a) Congress shall authorize personnel strength levels for each fiscal year for each of the following:

&emsp;(1) The end strength for each of the armed forces (other than the Coast Guard) for (A) active-duty personnel who are to be paid from funds appropriated for active-duty personnel, and (B) active-duty personnel and full-time National Guard duty personnel who are to be paid from funds appropriated for reserve personnel.

&emsp;(2) The end strength for the Selected Reserve of each reserve component of the armed forces.

&emsp;(3) The average military training student loads for each of the armed forces (other than the Coast Guard).

(b) No funds may be appropriated for any fiscal year to or for—

&emsp;(1) the use of active-duty personnel or full-time National Guard duty personnel of any of the armed forces (other than the Coast Guard) unless the end strength for such personnel of that armed force for that fiscal year has been authorized by law;

&emsp;(2) the use of the Selected Reserve of any reserve component of the armed forces unless the end strength for the Selected Reserve of that component for that fiscal year has been authorized by law; or

&emsp;(3) training military personnel in the training categories described in subsection (f) of any of the armed forces (other than the Coast Guard) unless the average student load of that armed force for that fiscal year has been authorized by law.

(c) Upon determination by the Secretary of Defense that such action is in the national interest, the Secretary may—

&emsp;(1) increase the end strength authorized pursuant to subsection (a)(1)(A) for a fiscal year for any of the armed forces by a number equal to not more than 0.5 percent of that end strength; and

&emsp;(2) increase the end strength authorized pursuant to subsection (a)(1)(B) for a fiscal year for any of the armed forces by a number equal to not more than 2 percent of that end strength.

(d) In counting active-duty personnel for the purpose of the end-strengths authorized pursuant to subsection (a)(1), persons in the following categories shall be excluded:

&emsp;(1) Members of the Ready Reserve ordered to active duty under section 673 <sup>1</sup> of this title.

&emsp;(2) Members of the Selected Reserve of the Ready Reserve ordered to active duty under section 673b <sup>1</sup> of this title.

&emsp;(3) Members of the National Guard called into Federal service under section 3500 <sup>1</sup> or 8500 <sup>1</sup> of this title.

&emsp;(4) Members of the militia called into Federal service under chapter 15 of this title.

&emsp;(5) Members of reserve components on active duty for training.

&emsp;(6) Members of reserve components on active duty for 180 days or less to perform special work.

&emsp;(7) Members on full-time National Guard duty for 180 days or less.

(e) The authorized strength of the Navy under subsection (a)(1) is increased by the authorized strength of the Coast Guard during any period when the Coast Guard is operating as a service in the Navy.

(f) Authorization under subsection (a)(3) is not required for unit or crew training student loads, but is required for student loads for the following individual training categories:

&emsp;(1) Recruit and specialized training.

&emsp;(2) Flight training.

&emsp;(3) Professional training in military and civilian institutions.

&emsp;(4) Officer acquisition training.

#### Minimum Number of Navy Health Professions Officers

Section 718(b) of Pub. L. 102–190 provided that: “Of the total number of officers authorized to be serving on active duty in the Navy on the last day of a fiscal year, 12,510 shall be available only for assignment to duties in health profession specialties.”

#### Limitations on Reductions in Medical Personnel

Section 711 of Pub. L. 101–510, as amended by Pub. L. 102–190, div. A, title VII, §718(a), Dec. 5, 1991, 105 Stat. 1404, provided that:

“(a)

Limitation on Reduction.—The Secretary of Defense may not reduce the number of medical personnel of the Department of Defense below the baseline number unless the Secretary of Defense—

&emsp;“(1) certifies to the Congress that—

&emsp;&emsp;“(A) the number of such personnel being reduced is excess to the current and projected needs of the military departments; and

&emsp;&emsp;“(B) such reduction will not result in an increase in the cost of health care services provided under the Civilian Health and Medical Program of the Uniformed Services; and

&emsp;“(2) in the case of military medical personnel, includes in the certification the information specified in subsection (b).

“(b)

Information Required.—A certification made by the Secretary of Defense in compliance with subsection (a)(2) shall include the following:

&emsp;“(1) The strength levels for the individual category of medical personnel involved in the reduction as of September 30, 1989.

&emsp;“(2) The projected requirements of the Department of Defense over the five-fiscal year period following the fiscal year in which the certification is submitted for medical personnel in the category of medical personnel involved in the reduction.

&emsp;“(3) The strength level recommended for each component of the Armed Forces for the most recent fiscal year for which the Secretary submitted recommendations pursuant to section 115a(g)(1) of title 10, United States Code (as added by section 1483), for personnel in the category of medical personnel involved in the reduction.

“(c)

Definitions.—For purposes of this section:

&emsp;“(1) The term ‘medical personnel’ has the meaning given that term in section 115a(g)(2) of title 10, United States Code (as added by section 1483), except that such term includes civilian personnel of the Department of Defense assigned to military medical facilities.

&emsp;“(2) The term ‘category of medical personnel’ means—

&emsp;&emsp;“(A) each corps referred to in subparagraphs (A) and (C) of section 115a(g)(2) of such title (as added by section 1483);

&emsp;&emsp;“(B) each designation referred to in subparagraph (B) of such section;

&emsp;&emsp;“(C) the enlisted personnel referred to in subparagraph (D) of such section; and

&emsp;&emsp;“(D) other medical personnel designated as medical personnel by the Secretary pursuant to subparagraph (E) of such section, if any.

&emsp;“(3) The term ‘baseline number’ means the number equal to the sum of 12,510 and the number of medical personnel of the Department of Defense serving on September 30, 1989, excluding commissioned officers of the Navy.”
