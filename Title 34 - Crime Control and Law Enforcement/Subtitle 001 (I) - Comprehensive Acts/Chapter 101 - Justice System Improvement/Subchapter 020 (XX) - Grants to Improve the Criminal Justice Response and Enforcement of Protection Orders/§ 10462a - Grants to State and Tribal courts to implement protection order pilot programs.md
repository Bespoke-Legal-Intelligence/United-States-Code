### §10462a. Grants to State and Tribal courts to implement protection order pilot programs

#### (a) Definition of eligible entity

In this section, the term "eligible entity" means a State or Tribal court that is part of a multidisciplinary partnership that includes, to the extent practicable—

&emsp;(1) a State, Tribal, or local law enforcement agency;

&emsp;(2) a State, Tribal, or local prosecutor's office;

&emsp;(3) a victim service provider or State or Tribal domestic violence coalition;

&emsp;(4) a provider of culturally specific services;

&emsp;(5) a nonprofit program or government agency with demonstrated experience in providing legal assistance or legal advice to victims of domestic violence and sexual assault;

&emsp;(6) the bar association of the applicable State or Indian Tribe;

&emsp;(7) the State or Tribal association of court clerks;

&emsp;(8) a State, Tribal, or local association of criminal defense attorneys;

&emsp;(9) not fewer than 2 individuals with expertise in the design and management of court case management systems and systems of integration;

&emsp;(10) not fewer than 2 State or Tribal court judges with experience in—

&emsp;&emsp;(A) the field of domestic violence; and

&emsp;&emsp;(B) issuing protective orders; and

&emsp;(11) a judge assigned to the criminal docket of the State or Tribal court.

#### (b) Grants authorized

#### (1) In general

&emsp;The Attorney General shall make grants to eligible entities to carry out the activities described in subsection (c) of this section.

#### (2) Number

&emsp;The Attorney General may award not more than 10 grants under paragraph (1).

#### (3) Amount

&emsp;The amount of a grant awarded under paragraph (1) may be not more than $1,500,000.

#### (c) Mandatory activities

#### (1) In general

&emsp;An eligible entity that receives a grant under this section shall use the grant funds, in consultation with the partners of the eligible entity described in subsection (a), to—

&emsp;&emsp;(A) develop and implement a program for properly and legally serving protection orders through electronic communication methods to—

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(i) modernize the service process and make the process more effective and efficient;

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(ii) provide for improved safety of victims; and

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(iii) make protection orders enforceable as quickly as possible;

&emsp;&emsp;(B) develop best practices relating to the service of protection orders through electronic communication methods;

&emsp;&emsp;(C) ensure that the program developed under subparagraph (A) complies with due process requirements and any other procedures required by law or by a court; and

&emsp;&emsp;(D) implement any technology necessary to carry out the program developed under subparagraph (A), such as technology to verify and track the receipt of a protection order by the intended party.

#### (2) Timeline

&emsp;An eligible entity that receives a grant under this section shall—

&emsp;&emsp;(A) implement the program required under paragraph (1)(A) not later than 2 years after the date on which the eligible entity receives the grant; and

&emsp;&emsp;(B) carry out the program required under paragraph (1)(A) for not fewer than 3 years.

#### (d) Diversity of recipients

The Attorney General shall award grants under this section to eligible entities in a variety of areas and situations, including, to the extent practicable—

&emsp;(1) a State court that serves a population of not fewer than 1,000,000 individuals;

&emsp;(2) a State court that—

&emsp;&emsp;(A) serves a State that is among the 7 States with the lowest population density in the United States; and

&emsp;&emsp;(B) has a relatively low rate of successful service with respect to protection orders, as determined by the Attorney General;

&emsp;(3) a State court that—

&emsp;&emsp;(A) serves a State that is among the 7 States with the highest population density in the United States; and

&emsp;&emsp;(B) has a relatively low rate of successful service with respect to protection orders, as determined by the Attorney General;

&emsp;(4) a court that uses an integrated, statewide case management system;

&emsp;(5) a court that uses a standalone case management system;

&emsp;(6) a Tribal court; and

&emsp;(7) a court that primarily serves a culturally specific and underserved population.

#### (e) Application

#### (1) In general

&emsp;An eligible entity desiring a grant under this section shall submit to the Attorney General an application that includes—

&emsp;&emsp;(A) a description of the process that the eligible entity uses for service of protection orders at the time of submission of the application;

&emsp;&emsp;(B) to the extent practicable, statistics relating to protection orders during the 3 calendar years preceding the date of submission of the application, including rates of—

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(i) successful service; and

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(ii) enforcement;

&emsp;&emsp;(C) an initial list of the entities serving as the partners of the eligible entity described in subsection (a); and

&emsp;&emsp;(D) any other information the Attorney General may reasonably require.

#### (2) No other application required

&emsp;An eligible entity shall not be required to submit an application under section 10462 of this title to receive a grant under this section.

#### (f) Report to Attorney General

#### (1) Initial report

&emsp;Not later than 2 years after the date on which an eligible entity receives a grant under this section, the eligible entity shall submit to the Attorney General a report that details the plan of the eligible entity for implementation of the program under subsection (c).

#### (2) Subsequent reports

#### (A) In general

&emsp;&emsp;Not later than 1 year after the date on which an eligible entity implements a program under subsection (c), and not later than 2 years thereafter, the eligible entity shall submit to the Attorney General a report that describes the program, including, with respect to the program—

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(i) the viability;

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(ii) the cost;

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(iii) service statistics;

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(iv) the challenges;

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(v) an analysis of the technology used to fulfill the goals of the program;

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(vi) an analysis of any legal or due process issues resulting from the electronic service method described in subsection (c)(1)(A); and

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(vii) best practices for implementing such a program in other similarly situated locations.

#### (B) Contents of final report

&emsp;&emsp;An eligible entity shall include in the second report submitted under subparagraph (A) recommendations for—

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(i) future nationwide implementation of the program implemented by the eligible entity; and

&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;(ii) usage of electronic service, similar to the service used by the eligible entity, for other commonly used court orders, including with respect to viability and cost.

#### (g) No regulations or guidelines required

Notwithstanding section 10464 of this title, the Attorney General shall not be required to publish regulations or guidelines implementing this section.

#### (h) Authorization of appropriations

There is authorized to be appropriated to carry out this section $10,000,000 for fiscal years 2023 through 2027.

(Pub. L. 90–351, title I, §2103, as added Pub. L. 117–103, div. W, title XV, §1506(2), Mar. 15, 2022, 136 Stat. 957.)
