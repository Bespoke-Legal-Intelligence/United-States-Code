### §601. Definitions

As used in this chapter—

&emsp;(1) the term “agency head” means the head and any assistant head of an executive agency, and may “upon the designation by” the head of an executive agency include the chief official of any principal division of the agency;

&emsp;(2) the term “executive agency” means an executive department as defined in section 101 of title 5, an independent establishment as defined by section 104 of title 5 (except that it shall not include the Government Accountability Office), a military department as defined by section 102 of title 5, and a wholly owned Government corporation as defined by section 9101(3) of title 31;

&emsp;(3) the term “contracting officer” means any person who, by appointment in accordance with applicable regulations, has the authority to enter into and administer contracts and make determinations and findings with respect thereto. The term also includes the authorized representative of the contracting officer, acting within the limits of his authority;

&emsp;(4) the term “contractor” means a party to a Government contract other than the Government;

&emsp;(5) the term “Administrator” means the Administrator for Federal Procurement Policy appointed pursuant to the Office of Federal Procurement Policy Act \[41 U.S.C. 401 et seq.\];

&emsp;(6) the terms “agency board” or “agency board of contract appeals” mean—

&emsp;&emsp;(A) the Armed Services Board of Contract Appeals established under section 607(a)(1) of this title;

&emsp;&emsp;(B) the Civilian Board of Contract Appeals established under section 42 of the Office of Federal Procurement Policy Act \[41 U.S.C. 438\];

&emsp;&emsp;(C) the board of contract appeals of the Tennessee Valley Authority; or

&emsp;&emsp;(D) the Postal Service Board of Contract Appeals established under section 607(c) of this title;

&emsp;(7) the term “Armed Services Board” means the Armed Services Board of Contract Appeals established under section 607(a)(1) of this title;

&emsp;(8) the term “Civilian Board” means the Civilian Board of Contract Appeals establishedunder section 42 of the Office of Federal Procurement Policy Act \[41 U.S.C. 438\]; and

&emsp;(9) the term “misrepresentation of fact” means a false statement of substantive fact, or any conduct which leads to a belief of a substantive fact material to proper understanding of the matter in hand, made with intent to deceive or mislead.

(Pub. L. 95–563, §2, Nov. 1, 1978, 92 Stat. 2383; Pub. L. 104–106, div. D, title XLIII, §4322(b)(5), Feb. 10, 1996, 110 Stat. 677; Pub. L. 108–271, §8(b), July 7, 2004, 118 Stat. 814; Pub. L. 109–163, div. A, title VIII, §847(d)(1), Jan. 6, 2006, 119 Stat. 3393.)

L. 95–563, which enacted this chapter, amended section 5108 of Title 5, Government Organization and Employees, section 1346, 1491, 2401, 2414, 2510, and 2517 of Title 28, Judiciary and Judicial Procedure, and section 724a of former Title 31, Money and Finance, and enacted provisions set out as notes under this section. Section 16 of Pub. L. 95–563 provided that: “This Act \[see Short Title note below\] shall apply to contracts entered into one hundred twenty days after the date of enactment \[Nov. 1, 1978\]. Notwithstanding any provision in a contract made before the effective date of this Act, the contractor may elect to proceed under this Act with respect to any claim pending then before the contracting officer or initiated thereafter.”
